04 / AI in practice

I do not present AI as a magic wand. I show the task, the output, and the human decision that made it useful.

AI is most useful to me when it makes a line of inquiry faster to test—not easier to assume.

I use it to widen options, reveal patterns worth investigating, and prototype conversations. I remain responsible for the framing, source material, evaluation, and final choice.

01

Set a bounded task

I define the question, relevant context, source constraints, and what a good output must enable.

02

Generate, compare, challenge

I use multiple prompts or tools to surface alternatives—not to manufacture certainty.

03

Verify with people and evidence

I check outputs against research, domain knowledge, and, whenever possible, real user feedback.

05 / About

I am drawn to problems that sit between individual experience and the systems shaping it.

Before design, I trained in civil engineering. It taught me to respect constraints, dependencies, and the consequences of small decisions at scale. Human-centred design taught me to ask who a system works for, who it leaves carrying the effort, and what should change.

Today, I bring both lenses to research, service, and product work. I am seeking an entry-level [Product Designer / UX Researcher / Service Designer] role where thoughtful process and practical outcomes matter.
